## 1. How big is the MLBB player base (2025–2026): MAU/DAU, downloads, countries, age, new vs returning, devices, revenue/ARPU?

### Takeaway
MLBB has roughly 105–110M monthly players. That is Moonton's claim, and AppMagic's estimates agree with it. It has about 30M daily players, gets about 95M new installs a year, and is heavily concentrated in Southeast Asia (SEA). Its app-store revenue is small for its size, though: about US$166M in 2025, or about US$1.6 per monthly player per year. Most players spend little or nothing in the stores. iOS is about one-fifth of MAU, and no source measures iPad use specifically.

- AppMagic monthly estimates: MAU of 104.6–106.0M from Jun to Dec 2025, rising to 107.2–109.9M from Jan to May 2026. DAU is 29.1–32.3M, which puts DAU/MAU at about 28.5%. Peak MAU was 112.3M. Article dated 1 Jul 2026 — [Udonis (AppMagic data)](https://www.blog.udonis.co/statistics/mobile-legends-bang-bang)

### Inferences
- **Store ARPU is very low.** $166M in 2025 ÷ about 105M average MAU ≈ **$1.58 per MAU per year, or about $0.13 per month**. That figure is net of store fees and excludes web top-ups such as Codashop and UniPin, which are common in SEA. The true spend per player is higher, but probably not by an order of magnitude. A US$2–3/month subscription would cost more than the average MLBB player spends on MLBB itself in a year. Only the small paying minority is a realistic target.
- **New-player inflow:** 94.5M downloads in 2025 ≈ 7.9M per month. Downloads include re-installs on new phones and returning players. If 30–50% are genuinely new players (my assumption), that is about **2.4–3.9M new players per month** worldwide.
- **The iPad requirement cuts the addressable pool hard.** iOS is about 21% of MAU, which is about 22M players. No source gives the iPad share. If 10–20% of iOS MLBB players use an iPad (assumption), the current setup can reach only about **2–4M players worldwide (about 2–4% of MAU)**. They also need a second phone, and every one of them must know how to build an iOS Shortcut. A phone-only or Android-capable flow would widen the reachable pool by about 5–20x.
- Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos rank high on interest but have low purchasing power. Indonesia, the Philippines and Malaysia are the realistic paying markets.

### Gaps
- There is no authoritative per-country MAU for the Philippines, Malaysia, Myanmar, Cambodia or Brazil. The only country figure, Indonesia's 35M, is from 2024.
- There is no data on the split between new and returning players, or on how many players are still in their first month.
- There is no data on how many MLBB players play on an iPad. StatCounter measures tablet web traffic, not tablet ownership, and its PH vendor data is unreliable because of the large "Unknown" share.
- No source covers revenue from web top-ups (Codashop, UniPin and similar), which AppMagic and Sensor Tower do not track. Sensor Tower's and AppMagic's revenue figures conflict.
- There is no 2025–2026 ARPU by country. The Sensor Tower SEA 2025 PDF tables were not reachable.

## 2. What do new players struggle with, and how much demand is there for beginner content?

### Takeaway
The evidence lines up with the product's features. New players struggle with too many heroes and fast-changing metas, with not counter-picking or adapting, with getting stuck in Epic, and with toxic or trolling teammates. However, the game already shows recommended builds and flashes the next item to buy, which weakens a plain "what to buy next" pitch. The product's advantage would be advice that adapts to the actual enemy team, not a generic build. Most first-hand complaints are about matchmaking and toxicity, which a coach cannot fix directly.

### Cited Findings
- Guides say it is overwhelming to sample every role each night, and that MLBB patches heroes roughly every 3–6 weeks, so the tier list keeps moving (snippet) — [PixelTwelve](https://pixeltwelve.com/articles/mobile-legends-bang-bang-beginner-guide-roles-lanes-objectives-ranked-play)
- A "comprehensive collection of Heroes may often feel overwhelming" for new players (snippet) — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/5-best-mobile-legends-heroes-beginners-2023)
- The in-game tutorial "barely covers several important aspects of gameplay" (snippet) — [Sportskeeda tips](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/5-tips-tricks-get-better-mobile-legends-bang-bang)
- On being stuck in Epic, one guide quotes a pro: "Most players get stuck because they only play one hero. They don't counter-pick or adapt." Guides describe Epic as the most populated rank, with a wide skill gap and common griefing, where solo queue "can be a nightmare". Listed causes include random drafting and trolling (snippet; the quotes come from one of these two guides, I could not confirm which) — [ONE Esports: rank up Epic](https://www.oneesports.gg/mobile-legends/how-to-rank-up-epic-mlbb/); [Carry1st low-rank guide](https://carry1st.com/blog/how-to-get-out-of-low-ranks-in-mlbb-the-ultimate-guide)
- 2025 player complaints on Sikayetvar:
  - A teammate idled and insulted the team during a ranked match — [Sikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/mobile-legends-us/i-lost-a-ranked-match-in-mobile-legends-due-to-a-toxic-teammate-who-sabotaged-the-game)
  - "for every win, I'm forced into a losing streak of five or six games" — [Sikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/mobile-legends-us/i-keep-losing-in-mlbb-because-matchmaking-pairs-me-with-clueless-toxic-players)
  - A player stuck in Epic despite good stats — [Sikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/mobile-legends-us/mobile-legends-matchmaking-keeps-me-stuck-in-epic-rank-despite-high-stats)
  - Reports that get only automated replies — [Sikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/mobile-legends-us/i-lost-my-star-in-mobile-legends-due-to-a-toxic-player-and-couldnt-file-a-complaint)
  - These are anecdotes, not a survey.
- What the game already does: it shows recommended builds in the top-right under the gold count, and "the system flashes the next item you have to buy to complete your build". Guides still tell players to check their hero's build "instead of just buying the recommended Items" (snippet) — [Codashop guide](https://news.codashop.com/my/?p=19592); [ONE Esports beginners guide](https://www.oneesports.gg/mobile-legends/mobile-legends-beginners-guide/)
- Beginner content demand: one 2026 video, "The BEGINNER'S GUIDE You Actually Need in Mobile Legends 2026" by Visor YT, shows about 460k views. That figure comes from a scraped aggregator and is unverified — [aggregator page](https://learn-python.programiz.com/z/fr/x7v/ml-guide/pages/). Many publishers keep beginner guides up to date, including ONE Esports, Codashop, PocketGamer and Coins.ph — [ONE Esports](https://www.oneesports.gg/mobile-legends/the-beginners-guide-to-getting-good-at-mobile-legends-bang-bang/); [Coins.ph Academy](https://coins.ph/academy/mobile-legends-beginner-guide-characters-ranks-mechanics)
- Ranked seasons last about 3 months and ranks reset each season. The Epic struggle therefore comes back every quarter (snippet) — [PocketGamer ranks](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/086102/mobile-legends-ranks)

### Inferences
- **Pain points the product addresses:** counter-picking (draft help), not adapting builds to the enemy team (live "buy next"), confusion over emblems and spells, and the "stuck in Epic because I can't adapt" problem.
- **Pain points it does not address:** toxic or AFK teammates, matchmaking fairness and broken reporting. These dominate the complaints I could find, and the product should not claim to fix them. It can still reduce blame, since a player who builds correctly gets flamed less.
- **The built-in recommended build is the main free substitute.** MLBB Guru has to show clearly that its advice changes with the enemy composition (for example anti-heal or magic defense) and that it is spoken aloud. Otherwise "what to buy next" duplicates the game's own prompt.
- The quarterly rank reset creates a recurring moment of need at the start of each season, when players climb out of Epic and Legend again. A seasonal pass would fit this rhythm.

### Gaps
- I could not pull current r/MobileLegendsGame threads. Searches returned only a 2021 archived post and third-party guides. No Facebook group or app-review sentiment data was reachable.
- I have no verified YouTube or TikTok view counts for "beginner guide" or "best build" content. YouTube search pages need JavaScript. The one figure above (about 460k views) is unverified.
- I found no survey quantifying what share of new players quit, or why.

## 3. Do players already pay for help: joki (boosting), coaching, tools?

### Takeaway
Yes, and the joki market is large and well established in Indonesia. Boosting is sold per star, at about Rp4,000–34,000 (US$0.25–2.00) per star depending on rank. Getting out of one tier costs roughly Rp175k–375k (US$10–22), even though Moonton bans boosted accounts. Western coaching marketplaces charge about US$8–24 per hour. Third-party counter-pick and build apps exist, but none shows proven scale. One web tool, mlbb.io, advertised itself for sale at only US$7,500. People pay to get results ("give me stars"). Paying for self-improvement is much less proven.

### Cited Findings
**Joki pricing (Indonesia, per star)**
- Lapakgaming: Epic Rp7,000; Legend Rp10,000; Mythic Rp20,000; Mythic Honor Rp24,000; Mythic Glory 50+ Rp29,000; Glory 100+ Rp34,000 per star (snippet; the page shows a Jul 2026 date) — [Lapakgaming joki](https://www.lapakgaming.com/id-id/joki-mlbb); [Lapakgaming guide](https://www.lapakgaming.com/blog/id-id/panduan-jasa-joki-ml)

**Existing tools (competitors)**
- MLBB Support: Counter & Stats is an unofficial app offering counters, builds, meta and a draft helper, with "100,000+" installs per an APK mirror — [APKCombo](https://apkcombo.com/mlbb-support-counter-stats/com.mlbb.support/)

### Inferences
- **Joki anchors willingness to pay to escape a rank.** Assuming 25 stars per tier (Epic V–I × 5 stars; my assumption): Epic to Legend via joki ≈ 25 × Rp7,000–15,000 = **Rp175k–375k (about US$10–22)**, and Legend to Mythic ≈ 25 × Rp10,000–15,000 = **Rp250k–375k (about US$15–22)**. A coach priced at Rp29k–49k per month (about US$1.7–2.9) is roughly one-sixth to one-tenth of a single joki tier, and it carries no ban risk. That is a strong positioning line: "climb it yourself for the price of 3 joki stars".
- Joki buyers want the outcome, not learning. Conversion from joki buyers will be partial, but some of them are clearly frustrated players with money to spend.
- Competing tools are free and ad-supported, or barely monetized. The apparent sale listing for mlbb.io (10k+ users, US$7.5k asking price) suggests draft and counter data alone is a weak, commoditized business. The live, spoken, screenshot-aware coach is the only differentiated, payable feature.
- Hourly coaching at US$8–24 is far more expensive than an AI coach would be. It supports pricing a "pro analysis" tier (for example a post-match review) above a basic subscription.

### Gaps
- There is no credible estimate of total joki market size in Indonesia or the Philippines. All the figures are anecdotal or self-reported.
- I found no data on paid MLBB Discord communities or how many members they have.
- I found no Philippine-specific joki ("boosting/piloting") price survey.
- I could not verify install counts for competitor apps on Google Play itself.
- Moonton's stance on third-party screen-reading or overlay coaching tools (terms-of-service risk) was not researched here. The joki bans show that Moonton enforces against rank manipulation.

## 4. How willing are SEA users to pay for apps and subscriptions, and how can they pay?

### Takeaway
Successful global subscriptions sell for about ₱169–189 per month in the Philippines and about Rp71k in Indonesia (about US$3–4). An MLBB tool for teenagers and young adults should price below that, at about ₱79–149 or Rp25k–49k per month, and offer weekly or per-match "sachet" options. E-wallets (GCash, Maya, DANA, GoPay, OVO, QRIS) are the main payment rails. Xendit, PayMongo and Midtrans charge about 1.5–2.7% on e-wallets, compared with about 3% plus a fixed fee on cards. Google Play takes 15% on subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
**Price benchmarks**
- Spotify Premium Philippines: Individual ₱169 per month (raised from ₱149 in Aug 2025), Duo ₱229, Family ₱279, Student ₱85 — [Spotify PH](https://www.spotify.com/ph-en/premium/); [Technobaboy, Aug 2025](https://www.technobaboy.com/2025/08/05/new-spotify-premium-prices-in-the-philippines-global-increases-coming-soon)

## 5. Which monetization models fit, and what revenue is realistic?

### Takeaway
The best fit is freemium. Draft, counter and emblem help should be free, because it is cheap to serve and commoditized. The live spoken coach should be paid through e-wallet weekly or seasonal passes and per-match credits. Affiliate top-up links and creator partnerships can add revenue on the side. On the current iPad + Shortcut setup, realistic revenue is in the hundreds of US dollars per month. Reaching low tens of thousands per month needs a much simpler, phone-first onboarding and creator-driven distribution.

### Cited Findings
- Codashop affiliate and ambassador program: creators "can earn up to 10% commission for every successful conversion" — [Codashop News](https://news.codashop.com/my/?p=24608). The rate on Involve Asia is 3.5% for new customers on most products — [Involve Asia](https://app.involve.asia/directory/codashop-affiliate-program). Cookie length is 30 days per FlexOffers and 7 days per Cuelinks, so sources conflict — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/codashop-affiliate-program); [Cuelinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/codashop-affiliate-program). Coda says the program rewards "thousands of affiliates" — [Coda press release](https://www.coda.co/press/it-pays-to-play-with-the-codashop-affiliate-program/)
- Free, unmonetized draft and counter tools already exist (mlbb.io, MLBB Support, Nova Counter). mlbb.io lists no paid plans — [mlbb.io](https://mlbb.io/en); [APKCombo](https://apkcombo.com/mlbb-support-counter-stats/com.mlbb.support/)
- Joki and coaching price anchors are in section 3; subscription price anchors are in section 4.
- Esports reach suggests creators and teams could work as B2B or co-marketing channels: the M7 Grand Final peaked at 5.68M concurrent viewers — [Esports.net](https://www.esports.net/news/mobile-games/mlbb-m7-world-championship-smashes-6-wild-records/)

### Inferences
**Model comparison (judgment, based on the evidence above)**

| Model | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Freemium (free draft and emblem plan; paid live coach) | **Best** | The draft step is a few calls per match and almost free (see section 6). The live coach is the differentiated, costly part. |
| Weekly, monthly or seasonal pass via e-wallet | **Strong** | Matches sachet buying habits and the 3-month ranked season. E-wallet fees are 1.5–2.7%. |
| Per-match credits | **Strong for cost control** | Directly caps heavy users, whose cost is roughly linear in matches (section 6). Easy to explain ("₱5 per coached match"). |
| Ads | Weak | Page views at match time are low (the user is playing), and SEA ad rates are low. Could cover free-tier draft users at best. No ad-rate data was gathered. |
| Affiliate (Codashop/UniPin top-up links) | Small add-on | 3.5–10% commission on diamond top-ups. Average store spend is only about $1.6 per player per year, so the payout per user is cents. |
| B2B (esports academies, school leagues, guilds) | Niche, high effort | Plausible for team draft analytics, but these buyers want deep stats, not beginner coaching. No pricing data was found. |
| White-label for streamers and creators | Promising for distribution | Creator-branded "coach" plus a revenue share could solve acquisition, which is the hardest problem. Unproven. |
| BYOK (bring your own Gemini key, current setup) | Zero cost, but high friction | Fine for power users. Kills mainstream adoption and makes the product hard to charge for. |

**Revenue scenarios (my assumptions; price about US$1.7/month net, ₱99 or Rp29k):**
- *Current iPad + Shortcut + BYOK setup:* reachable pool about 2–4M players (section 1). If 0.25% try it and 2% of those pay: 5,000–10,000 users × 2% = **100–200 payers, about US$170–340 per month**.
- *Simplified phone-first web app, organic growth:* 50,000 MAU × 3% = **1,500 payers, about US$2,500 per month (about US$30k per year)**.
- *Creator-driven breakout:* 300,000 MAU × 4% = **12,000 payers, about US$20,000 per month (about US$245k per year)**. That is about 0.3% of MLBB MAU, aggressive but not absurd given 5.68M esports peak viewers.
- Sanity check: mlbb.io had 10k+ claimed users, no paid tier and a US$7.5k asking price. Without the paid live-coach feature, the realistic value is low.

### Gaps
- I found no public conversion-rate benchmarks for gaming companion apps in SEA, so the 2–4% conversion rates above are assumptions.
- I found no data on ad rates (CPM) for SEA gaming web traffic.
- I found no evidence on what esports academies or school leagues (for example MSC or school MPL programs) pay for software.
- I did not research UniPin or Moogold affiliate rates.

## 6. Unit economics: Gemini API cost per match and per user, hosting, and free-tier limits

### Takeaway
On a Flash-Lite model, one coached match costs about **US$0.01–0.05** at one screenshot every 60s or every 15s. At 3 matches a day, a user costs about **US$1–4.50 per month** on Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite. That is fine at 1 per 60s and loses money at 1 per 15s against a ₱99 (about US$1.7) price. The newer "Flash" models cost 3–6x more, and their prices double on 1 Jan 2027. Hosting on Vercel is negligible if images go straight from the device to Gemini. The free tier (about 500 requests per day on Flash-Lite) covers about 8–26 matches a day per user key, which is why the BYOK setup works today.

### Cited Findings
**Gemini API prices** (USD per 1M tokens, Standard tier; output includes thinking tokens) — [Gemini API pricing (ai.google.dev, now redirects to aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)
- Gemini 3.8 Flash: input $0.75 / output $3.75 through 31 Dec 2026, rising to **$1.50 / $7.50 from 1 Jan 2027**. Gemini 3.6 Flash has the same pricing.
- Gemini 3 Flash Preview: $0.50 / $3.00.
- Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite: $0.30 / $2.50 (batch $0.15 / $1.25).
- Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite: $0.25 / $1.50 (batch $0.125 / $0.75).
- Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite: $0.10 / $0.40.
- Gemini 2.5 Flash: $0.30 / $2.50. It is **deprecated, and from 1 Dec 2026 requests to it route to 3.5 Flash-Lite**, billed at that model's rates.
- All the models above have a free tier. Free-tier data is "used to improve our products"; paid-tier data is not.
- TTS: Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS charges $0.50 per 1M input tokens and $6.00 per 1M audio output tokens (doubling in 2027). Audio is 25 tokens per second. The page conflicts on whether TTS has a free tier: the TTS table says "Yes", while the free-tier notes say Gemini TTS is "Not available".

**Image token counts**
- Gemini 3.x models: `media_resolution` low = 280 tokens per image, medium = 560, high = 1,120, ultra_high = 2,240. **The default (unspecified) is 1,120** — [Media resolution docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/media-resolution)
- Older tiling rule: images of 384px or less cost 258 tokens. Larger images are tiled into 768×768 tiles at 258 tokens each, using a crop unit of about floor(min side ÷ 1.5). A 1600×900 image works out to about 6 tiles, or **about 1,548 tokens**, and a 1600×1200 image to about 4 tiles, or about 1,032 tokens — [Image understanding docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/image-understanding)

**Rate limits and free tier**
- Official docs point to AI Studio for per-model free-tier limits. Limits apply per project, and the requests-per-day (RPD) count resets at midnight Pacific time. Tier 1 has a spend cap of $10 per rolling 10 minutes — [Rate limits docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/rate-limits)
- Third-party figures (Oct 2026): free tier of **500 RPD for 3.5 Flash-Lite and 3.1 Flash-Lite** (3.5 Flash-Lite also has 10 requests per minute and 250k tokens per minute), and **only 20 RPD for 3.8 and 3.6 Flash** — [ScriptByAI](https://www.scriptbyai.com/gemini-api-free-tier-limits/). A developer forum thread confirms the 20 RPD complaint for 3.8 Flash — [Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-8-flash-free-tier-20-rpd-is-too-limited-for-practical-evaluation/180609)

**Vercel**
- Hobby is $0 with hard caps: 1M function invocations, 4 hours of active CPU, 360 GB-hours of memory and 100 GB of data transfer per month, with no option to buy more.
- Pro is $20 per month, including $20 of usage credit. It includes 1 TB of data transfer, then about $0.15 per GB. Invocations cost $0.60 per 1M, active CPU starts at $0.128 per hour, and requests beyond the included 10M cost $2 per 1M. Pro usage is uncapped pay-as-you-go — [Vercel pricing](https://vercel.com/pricing)

### Inferences (cost model, arithmetic shown)
**Assumptions:**
- Each call sends 1 screenshot plus about 800 text tokens (system prompt and game state), and returns about 200 output tokens (short JSON or a spoken sentence, with thinking minimized).
- Match length is 15 min = 900 s, so 1 shot every 15s = 60 calls and 1 shot every 60s = 15 calls.
- Each match adds 4 calls for draft, loading-screen and emblem planning.
- A typical user plays 3 matches a day × 30 days = 90 matches per month.

| Model / image setting | Cost per call | Per match @1/15s (64 calls) | Per match @1/60s (19 calls) | Per user-month @1/15s | Per user-month @1/60s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 Flash-Lite, 1600×900 (~1,548 tokens) | $0.00031 | $0.020 | $0.006 | **$1.81** | **$0.54** |
| 3.1 Flash-Lite, low res (280) | $0.00057 | $0.037 | $0.011 | $3.28 | $0.97 |
| 3.1 Flash-Lite, default (1,120) | $0.00078 | $0.050 | $0.015 | **$4.49** | **$1.33** |
| 3.5 Flash-Lite, low (280) | $0.00082 | $0.053 | $0.016 | $4.75 | $1.41 |
| 3.5 Flash-Lite, default (1,120) | $0.00108 | $0.069 | $0.020 | $6.20 | $1.84 |
| 3 Flash Preview, default | $0.00156 | $0.100 | $0.030 | $8.99 | $2.67 |
| 3.8 Flash, default (2026 price) | $0.00219 | $0.140 | $0.042 | $12.61 | $3.74 |
| 3.8 Flash, default (2027 price) | $0.00438 | $0.280 | $0.083 | $25.23 | $7.49 |

Worked example for 3.1 Flash-Lite at default resolution: (1,120 + 800) × $0.25/1M = $0.00048 input, plus 200 × $1.50/1M = $0.00030 output, for $0.00078 per call. × 64 calls = $0.050 per match, × 90 matches = $4.49 per month.

- **Sensitivity to thinking tokens:** If the model "thinks" for about 1,000 tokens per call, 3.1 Flash-Lite costs about $0.0015 more per call, or +$0.096 per match at 1/15s. That roughly triples the cost. Thinking must be disabled or capped for the live loop.
- **Margin at ₱99 (about US$1.7) per month:**
  - At 1/60s on 3.1 Flash-Lite ($0.97–1.33 per month), the gross margin is thin but positive, at about 20–40% after e-wallet fees.
  - At 1/15s ($3.3–4.5), every paying user loses money.
  - A heavy user (10 matches a day) at 1/60s on default resolution costs about $4.4 per month.
  - Therefore: (a) capture on events (shop open, death, gold thresholds), not on a fixed interval; (b) crop to the item and gold areas and use `media_resolution: low`; (c) use per-match credits or caps for heavy users; (d) price the live coach at ₱149–199 or Rp49k if the 1/15s cadence is required.
- **Legacy-model risk:** 2.5 Flash-Lite is the cheapest option (about $0.54–1.81 per month) but belongs to a model family already being deprecated, since 2.5 Flash routes to 3.5 Flash-Lite from 1 Dec 2026. Plan on 3.1 or 3.5 Flash-Lite pricing.
- **Draft helper cost:** about 4 calls per match ≈ $0.003 per match on 3.1 Flash-Lite, or about $0.28 per user per month at 90 matches. Cheap enough to give away. With 10,000 free users at 1 match a day, that is about $940 per month, so even the free tier needs a daily cap.
- **Spoken output:** device-native speech (iOS "Speak Text" or the browser Web Speech API) costs nothing. Gemini TTS at about 3 seconds per tip ≈ 75 audio tokens ≈ $0.00045 per tip on 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS (2026 price). At 15 tips per match that adds about $0.007 per match, or about $0.6 per user per month. Use on-device TTS.
- **BYOK free tier:** at 500 RPD on Flash-Lite, a free key covers 500 ÷ 64 ≈ **7.8 matches per day at 1/15s**, or 500 ÷ 19 ≈ **26 matches per day at 1/60s**. 1/15s is 4 requests per minute, well under the 10 RPM limit. On 3.8 Flash's 20 RPD, a free key covers only about 1 match per day at 1/60s. Free-tier screenshots may be used by Google to improve its products, which is a privacy note to disclose.
- **Hosting (Vercel Pro):**
  - If screenshots of about 250 KB are proxied through Vercel functions, one user-month is 90 × 64 = 5,760 invocations (about $0.003) and about 1.41 GB of transfer at 1/15s, or 0.42 GB at 1/60s.
  - The included 1 TB covers about 730 users at 1/15s or about 2,450 at 1/60s. Beyond that, about $0.21 or $0.06 per user-month in transfer, assuming both directions count as Fast Data Transfer (not verified).
  - Active CPU is minimal, since time spent waiting on Gemini is not active CPU.
  - Sending images directly from the client to Gemini, using short-lived tokens issued by the backend, keeps hosting near the $20 base.
  - Hosting is therefore an order of magnitude below Gemini costs.

### Gaps
- I could not access AI Studio's live per-model rate-limit table. The 500 and 20 RPD figures are from a third party (Oct 2026), and Google changes free-tier quotas often (forum complaints mention cuts).
- I did not check whether Vercel counts inbound uploads as Fast Data Transfer, or the Hobby plan's commercial-use rule. The pricing page does not say.
- I did not measure real token counts for actual MLBB screenshots (for example a 1600×1200 iPad screenshot on Gemini 3.x at default resolution), or real output lengths. Use the `countTokens` endpoint and usage metadata to replace the assumptions above.
- I did not research Vertex AI pricing (enterprise or regional), which may differ from the Developer API.
